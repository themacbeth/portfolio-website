The Cosmology

The Kolob Theorem

KOLOB imagines the Milky Way as a single ordered cosmos — a structure of light read straight from the night sky and rendered as living code. One rule governs the whole field: everything turns about the centre, and everything is in its place.

Kolob — the core

At the dead centre sits Kolob: the first and greatest star, fixed while all else moves. In this rendering the centre is a black hole that swallows spent worlds and casts new ones out — as one world passes away, another comes. It is the brightest source in the sky at every wavelength, and the still point the entire galaxy rotates around.

Everything turns about the centre; the centre turns about nothing. — the only law of the chart

The Fifteen Fixed Stars

Three great stars at the heart work in affinity, bound to one another by beams of light, and a ring of twelve more turns with them as a single rigid solid — fifteen fixed stars in all, about which every other body orbits. KOLOB seals these as its Core 15: three particle suns and twelve governing systems, identical across the collection and impossible to equal.

The Three Kingdoms

The first colour photographs of nearby galaxies revealed three concentric rings — a white hub, a red ring, an outer blue ring. KOLOB reads these as the three kingdoms of light: Celestial at the dense gold core, Terrestrial in the middle, and Telestial on the sparse rim where our own sun would sit. Young blue stars gather at the edge; ancient red stars burn at the centre — the place of the first creations.

The Two Veils

Between the kingdoms lie veils of interstellar dust — the same dust that hides a galactic core from any telescope, gathered at each boundary. They wall off the light of the inner kingdoms from the outer. In the collection, advancing a throne inward is a passage through the veil.

The Migration of a World

A single world is alive in the chart, and it speaks. It is first formed in orbit of Kolob, migrates outward through the terrestrial ring in a long green age, and falls at last to a cold telestial exile on the rim. One day it is drawn back — through the veil, through the middle ring again, and home to its native orbit about the core.

I am pained, the world says, and asks only to rest. — the voice of the migrating world

Deep Time, and the Speed of Thought

The clocks here keep a slower time: one revolution of Kolob is a thousand of our years. Messengers cross the whole field in an instant — faster than light, at the speed of thought — and at the end of the longest cycle, there is time no longer.

Outer Darkness

Beyond the outermost ring, past the final veil, lies the void — no light, no heat, no stars. It is the place of the cast-out, and it churns at the rim of every chart.
